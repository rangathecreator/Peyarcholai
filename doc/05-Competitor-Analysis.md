## Competitor groups

### A. Baby-name websites / parenting portals
| Product | Focus | Strengths (general) | Gaps for our idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| BabyCenter (India edition) | Parenting content + name finder | Brand trust, big traffic, content | Weak regional/community depth, no numerology evaluation |
| Nameberry / Babynames.com | Western-centric name databases | Good UX, trends, popularity data | Little Indian community/language coverage |
| MomJunction | Indian parenting portal with large name lists | SEO reach, Indian-name lists by religion/letter | Ad-heavy pages, shallow evaluation, no family match |
| FirstCry Parenting | Parenting content tied to e-commerce | Audience of Indian parents | Name tool secondary to commerce |
| Parenting media (ParentCircle, Tinystep and similar) | Content + lists | Content marketing | Not a dedicated name product |

### B. Astrology / numerology platforms
| Product | Focus | Strengths | Gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| AstroSage | Astrology, name-by-nakshatra, numerology | Large Indian audience, multi-language | Astrology-first, little linguistic analysis, paid consultations upsell |
| Drik Panchang | Panchang and Hindu calendar tools | Trusted reputation for calendar accuracy | Not a name product |
| Astrotalk / Astroyogi and similar | Paid astrologer consultations | Marketplace revenue model | Different value proposition |
| Many standalone "name numerology" calculators and apps | Single-method calculators | Simple | Usually one method, no explanation of source, low trust |

### C. Mobile apps (Play Store / App Store)
- Many "Hindu baby names", "Muslim baby names", "Indian baby names" apps exist, typically ad-supported with static name lists.
- Typical gaps: no evaluation scorecard, no family match, no social feedback loop, poor regional-language coverage.

## Differentiation opportunities
1. **Evaluation scorecard** (pronunciation, spelling risk, family fit) – none of the typical competitors offer a full set.
2. **Transparent numerology** – show method, formula and source rather than a black-box verdict.
3. **"Remove-a-name" poll** – novel social loop.
4. **Community depth** – smaller communities and regional languages are poorly served.
5. **Trust** – cite verified sources for meaning and references.

## Threats
- Established portals with huge SEO traffic can add similar features.
- Astrology platforms have strong monetisation via consultations and could bundle name tools.
- Free generic apps set a low price expectation.
- AI chatbots can already suggest and explain names for free; our moat must be curated data, trust and the social/family workflow.
